The Hidden Friction in Structural Engineering—and Why the Market Is Ripe for Change

By Pourya Bazargani

Structural engineers sit at the fulcrum of every building project. Their calculations determine whether a design soars elegantly skyward or fails to meet code, schedule, or budget. Yet behind the high-stakes responsibility lies a day-to-day workflow burdened by legacy software, tight timelines, and razor-thin margins.

1. Racing the Clock

For a typical mid-rise residential project, engineers are allotted three to five weeks from receiving the architect's drawings to issuing stamped structural plans. Any delay stalls site prep and can cost a contractor $600–700 per day in idle labor and equipment. Under that pressure, engineers often prioritize speed over optimization—accepting heavier steel sections simply to keep construction moving.

2. Repetition Overload

Industry surveys show that over 50 % of engineers spend two or more hours per day on repetitive digital tasks: defining load cases, assigning member properties, exporting drawings, and re-formatting reports to meet client templates. These hours are non-billable on fixed-fee contracts, directly eroding project margins.

3. Onboarding and Usability Gaps

Tools like SAP2000 and ETABS are powerful but notoriously complex. New hires can take several months to reach full productivity, and every major software update triggers another learning cycle. 91 % of engineers report frustration with enterprise UX, and 20 % of the workweek can vanish to searching menus or re-creating lost macros.

4. Lost Optimization

Because optimization studies require iterative modeling—running dozens of what-if scenarios—teams under deadline often skip them. The result is widespread over-design: beams sized safely but conservatively, foundations cast larger than necessary, and developers paying for material the building doesn't need. Studies estimate 5–15 % of structural steel cost could be saved via systematic optimization, but the time just isn't there.

Market Size: An Underserved $70M–$500M Opportunity in the U.S. Alone

60,000 licensed structural engineers work in the United States.

At a conservative $1,200 per engineer per year for productivity software, the immediate structural-engineering spending potential is ~$72 million annually.

Broaden to all AEC professionals—civil, mechanical, and electrical engineers who face similar workflow pain—and the U.S. opportunity exceeds $500 million per year.

Global demand pushes that number easily into the billions, particularly as emerging markets adopt stricter building codes and digital design standards.

Despite the size of this spend, most dollars still flow to decades-old point solutions that focus on analysis, not the meta-workflow that drains so much engineering capacity.

Enter Compass: An AI Overlay Built for the Real Workflow

Compass tackles the bottlenecks where they hurt most:

Time Compression – By overlaying existing tools, Compass drives SAP2000 or ETABS through a single prompt, trimming design cycles from weeks to minutes.

Repetitive Task Automation – Screen-level AI completes load combos, sheet tagging, and report formatting automatically, reclaiming the two hours engineers lose each day.

In-Context Optimization – After the baseline design is done, Compass iterates beam and column sizes in the background, routinely delivering 5–15 % material savings without manual what-ifs.

Zero Integration Risk – Installs as a desktop EXE; no APIs, no data leaving the machine—meaning IT buys in quickly and engineers keep the tools they know.

In short, Compass removes the hidden friction points that have quietly capped structural-engineering productivity for decades. The profession doesn't need a brand-new platform; it needs an intelligent layer that lets engineers focus on engineering again.
